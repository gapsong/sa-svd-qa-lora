# Ablation note: initialization mechanism and seed robustness (2026-06-04/05)

The 2026-06-03 results were single-seed, and the run note's mechanism claim
("the principal-component structure gives optimization a better direction")
was an interpretation, not a measurement. Both were stress-tested with a
four-arm initialization ablation and a 3-seed sweep on SmolLM2-1.7B, plus two
structural probes. This note records what survived. The full adversarial log,
including registered predictions (2 of 4 were falsified), lives in
`research/red-team.md` on the `auto-research` branch.

Conditions: identical to the main runs (2-bit GPTQ, rank 16, group_size 16,
750 steps, lr 1e-4, WikiText-2 eval). All sweep runs used per-device batch 2 x
grad-accum 8; the seed-0 baseline used 4 x 4, but its seed-1 rerun at 2 x 8
reproduced it to 0.01 PPL, so batch shape is negligible.

## The four arms

- **baseline**: quantize W, PEFT default adapter init (A Kaiming, B = 0; the
  adapter contributes nothing at step 0).
- **sa_svd**: quantize the residual, adapter = SVD directions with SVD
  singular values.
- **random_matched**: quantize a residual built from random orthonormal
  directions carrying SA-SVD's exact singular values (per-layer norms match
  SA-SVD exactly; only the directions differ).
- **random_flat**: same random directions, but a flat spectrum (all component
  scales equal, product norm matched per layer).

A structural point makes these arms comparable: the subtracted component is
constant within each quantization group, and group-wise asymmetric
quantization absorbs per-group constants into the zero-point (Q(W - c) =
Q(W) - c). So every arm starts from an equivalent quantized base to first
order, and the arms differ only in adapter parameterization. (Verified
numerically: the residual quantizes bitwise-identically to W under a 2-bit
per-group RTN proxy, across all 168 target layers.)

## Results (WikiText-2 PPL, lower is better)

| arm | seed 0 | seed 1 | seed 2 | mean | spread |
|-----|--------|--------|--------|------|--------|
| baseline | 42.45 | 42.44 | 38.24 | 41.04 | 4.21 |
| sa_svd | 36.02 | 35.61 | 35.99 | 35.87 | 0.41 |
| random_matched | 35.65 | 38.31 | 36.53 | 36.83 | 2.66 |
| random_flat | 38.45 | 39.12 | 40.11 | 39.23 | 1.66 |

## Findings

1. **SA-SVD wins all 9 of 9 seed pairings against the baseline.** Worst
   sa_svd seed (36.02) vs best baseline seed (38.24): margin 2.2 PPL. Mean
   improvement 41.04 to 35.87, about -13%. The earlier single-seed "-15%"
   should not be quoted as a point estimate: the baseline's own seed spread
   (4.2 PPL) is two-thirds of that gap.
2. **SA-SVD makes the outcome nearly deterministic.** Seed spread 0.41 vs
   the baseline's 4.21, about 10x less, from a fully deterministic init.
3. **The singular values (spectrum) carry a consistent gain.**
   random_matched and random_flat share identical directions within each
   seed, so their difference isolates the spectrum: +2.80, +0.81, +3.58 PPL
   in seeds 0/1/2. Three of three positive. Concentrated SVD-shaped scale
   beats flat scale of equal total size.
4. **The singular vectors (directions) buy reliability, not a unique
   optimum.** Random directions with the SA-SVD spectrum span 35.65-38.31
   across three draws; SA-SVD's deterministic directions sit at 35.87 +/-
   0.2, at the top of that range every time.
5. **Scale alone is not enough.** random_flat's margin over the baseline
   mean (1.8 PPL) sits inside the baseline's own seed spread.
6. **A fifth arm (residual base + zero-contribution random adapter) does
   not train at all** (PPL 2525, inf gradients at every step): the benefit
   requires the init to preserve the step-0 function, which all
   group-constant constructions above do. The same gradient-overflow
   signature is how the Qwen2-1.5B random-init baseline fails, and a
   truncated Qwen2 run (588/750 clean steps before an external kill) shows
   the rescue also needs no SVD directions, only the scaled init.

## Corrected claims (supersede the 2026-06-03 phrasing)

- Supported: "SA-SVD initialization improves mean WikiText PPL by about 13%
  on SmolLM2 (3 seeds, 9/9 pairings, worst-case margin 2.2 PPL) and reduces
  run-to-run spread about 10x. The SVD's singular values carry a consistent
  paired gain; its singular vectors provide reliability. The benefit is an
  optimization effect of the adapter's initialization, not a difference in
  the quantized base, which is provably equivalent to first order."
- Retired: "the principal-component structure in the adapter gives
  optimization a better direction even from a worse starting point" (the
  direction part is too strong, the worse-starting-point part is not
  load-bearing).
- Still single-seed: the TinyLlama (-17%) and Qwen2 (trains vs does not
  train) rows of the main table.
